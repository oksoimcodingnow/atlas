The AI Ripple

Everyone buys NVIDIA. The sharper question: what does NVIDIA need — and what does that need? Trace the consequences outward and you find the un-obvious beneficiaries: power, grid gear, cooling, copper. Tap any node to expand the logic, the companies, and the risk.

⚠ The master risk — what breaks the whole chain

Every node here rests on one assumption: AI capex keeps growing. The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) are spending hundreds of billions on the bet that AI pays off. If that ROI disappoints, they cut spending fast — and the further out on the ripple you are (cooling, copper, uranium), the harder you fall, because your demand was derived from theirs. Less-crowded ≠ safer. It often means more indirect, and more fragile if the driver stalls.

How to think in ripples

1Trace the consequence. For anything booming, ask "and then what does THAT need?" Repeat. GPUs need power; power needs turbines, copper, uranium; dense chips need cooling.
2Count the order. 1st-order (NVIDIA) is obvious and crowded. Edge hides in the 2nd/3rd-order links most people haven't connected yet.
3Check what's priced in. The green nodes aren't "better" — they're less discovered, so your research can actually find a gap between price and value.
4Find the bottleneck. The scarce link in any chain (EUV, HBM, power, transformers, copper) has pricing power. Scarcity = moat.
5Name the risk that breaks it. Every thesis dies somewhere. Here it's AI capex stalling. Know your kill-switch before you buy.

This is a thesis map, not a recommendation. It exists to generate ideas to research — trace, then verify. Tickers are illustrative examples of exposure, not picks; many beneficiaries are non-US (SK Hynix, Siemens Energy, ABB) or private. Before acting on anything, run the valuation and demand a margin of safety. And remember: nobody reliably predicts short-term direction — the edge is buying durable exposure below its worth, then waiting.
