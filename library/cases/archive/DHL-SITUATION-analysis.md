## 0. THE SITUATION IN ONE PARAGRAPH
DHL Express Thailand is a market-leading international courier (1,200+ staff, ฿11,000M+ revenue, 220
countries, 36 ServicePoints) with **genuinely strong, first-mover green-logistics credentials** (first
in Thailand to run EVs commercially, SAF in planes, solar facilities, the GoGreen Plus carbon-reduction
service). Customers at the counter are **highly satisfied (83%)** — but that satisfaction is built on
*service quality*, not sustainability. There is a **perception gap**: the green effort is real but
*invisible at the point of sale*. The task is not to build green credibility (it exists) but to **make
customers aware they are already part of it** — within strict Green-Claims legal limits, on a ฿5M / 3-month
budget across 36 branches, with no celebrities.

## 1. THE 5C ANALYSIS (Company · Customers · Competitors · Collaborators · Context)

### 1A. COMPANY (DHL Express Thailand)
- **Scale & strength:** market leader, 220 countries, 1–3 day delivery, dedicated cargo aircraft.
- **Core product:** Time Definite International (TDI) — scheduled-time cross-border express.
- **Green assets (real, provable):**
  - GoGreen Plus (customer-facing carbon reduction via SAF / insetting) — *the key asset, customer-usable*
  - EVs: TH 43 (18.9%); global 50,604 (46.2%); first commercial EV logistics in TH (2022)
  - SAF now 10% of plane fuel; solar on 13 TH sites (92%); global buildings 96.9% renewable
  - Targets: 40M → <29M tonnes GHG by 2030, Net Zero 2050
- **Brand:** Bright / Focused / Energetic; values incl. Sustainable + Simplifying + Committed.
- **Honest internal weakness:** has *done* the work but *not communicated* it at the customer touchpoint.

### 1B. CUSTOMERS (who ships abroad at ServicePoints)
Four personas (case p14). Mindset differs sharply — this matters for targeting:
| Persona | Frequency | Green mindset | Reachability | Strategic value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Micro SMEs / online sellers** | High (repeat) | Pragmatic; green = a *selling point* for their shop | High (LINE, seller groups) | ⭐ **Highest** — repeat + own audience (multiplier) |
| **Expats** | Medium | Often eco-aware (EU norms) | Medium (niche groups) | Good secondary |
| **Students** | Low–Med | Eco-conscious generation, share online | Med (campuses, social) | Good for shareability |
| **Tourists** | One-time | Variable | Low (transient) | Weak — no repeat/relationship |
- **Shared truth:** they come for *speed & reliability*, not sustainability. Green is a bonus they don't
  yet know they're getting. → The message must ride *on top of* the existing satisfaction, not replace it.

### 1C. COMPETITORS / COMPETITIVE CONTEXT
- Direct express rivals (FedEx, UPS, local players) also pursue sustainability, but the case notes DHL is
  a **first-mover in TH commercial EV** and has a *customer-usable* product (GoGreen Plus). That's a
  differentiator most rivals communicate poorly too.
- **The real "competitor" is attention/indifference**, not another courier: at the counter, sustainability
  competes with the customer's actual goal (get my parcel sent fast). The campaign's job is to win a
  *sliver of attention* at that moment without slowing the transaction.
- **Opportunity:** if DHL *owns* the "you helped the planet" feeling at the counter, it claims a position
  rivals have left open.

### 1D. COLLABORATORS (who can amplify — no celebrities allowed)
- **SME sellers themselves** (turn customers into broadcasters — the multiplier)
- ServicePoint staff (free, human, point-of-sale influence)
- Online seller communities / marketplaces; universities/student orgs; expat groups
- Existing owned channels: LINE OA (DHL Express Shop), FB/IG (DHL Express Thailand), in-branch screens/posters

### 1E. CONTEXT (the macro forces)
- **Regulatory:** green marketing is under tightening law (e.g. EU Empowering Consumers Directive,
  referenced in the case Appendix). Claims must be specific, provable, non-misleading. This *constrains*
  the campaign but also *rewards* a team that respects it.
- **Cultural:** rising TH consumer awareness of sustainability, esp. among young + urban (the personas).
- **Behavioral:** "green fatigue / skepticism" is real — vague green claims are distrusted. Specificity wins.

## 2. THE CORE PROBLEM — A PROBLEM TREE (root → trunk → symptom)

```
SYMPTOM (what's measured)
   Low awareness of DHL's sustainability among ServicePoint customers
        ▲
TRUNK (the gap)
   The green effort is real but INVISIBLE at the moment of shipping
        ▲
ROOTS (the causes)
   1. No sustainability message at the point of sale (receipt/screen/staff say nothing about it)
   2. GoGreen Plus benefit isn't surfaced to walk-in customers
   3. Customers come for speed; green isn't on their radar at the counter
   4. Green Claims law makes DHL cautious → it under-communicates rather than risk a misstep
```
**Implication:** fix the *roots* (surface the message at point of sale, compliantly) and the symptom
(awareness) follows. This is why "point-of-sale felt moment" beats a generic awareness ad.

## 3. SWOT (sharp, not generic)

**Strengths**
- Real, first-mover, provable green credentials (defensible vs. greenwashing claims)
- A *customer-usable* green product (GoGreen Plus) — rare; gives customers an actual role
- 83% satisfaction + trusted brand → a warm audience already at the counter
- Owned touchpoints (36 branches, screens, LINE, staff, every receipt)

**Weaknesses**
- The green story is untold at the exact moment it matters (point of sale)
- Sustainability isn't why customers come → low baseline attention
- Legal caution → historically under-communicated

**Opportunities**
- Convert existing satisfaction into *pride* ("I love the service AND I helped the planet")
- SME multiplier: customers as broadcasters (earned reach, free)
- Own the open positioning rivals communicate poorly
- Specificity-as-trust: turn the legal constraint into a credibility *advantage*

**Threats**
- Greenwashing perception / legal misstep if claims aren't precise
- Green skepticism / fatigue among consumers
- Message ignored at a transactional moment (attention scarcity)
- 3-month / ฿5M / no-celeb limits cap reach

## 4. STAKEHOLDER MAP (who must care, and what they want)
- **Walk-in customer** → wants fast, easy shipping; *gettable* extra: feeling good about it.
- **SME seller** → wants to sell more; green badge = a selling point they'll actively spread.
- **ServicePoint staff** → need a simple line to say; low effort, high influence.
- **DHL brand/legal** → needs claims airtight; rewards compliant specificity.
- **The judges (UOB/DHL)** → want insight + feasibility + measurable ROI + storytelling + Green-Claims literacy.

## 5. THE AWARENESS → ACTION FUNNEL (where we're losing people, where to intervene)
```
Aware DHL is green?        ✗ LOW   ← the campaign's primary target
   ↓
Understand THEY are part of it?   ✗ LOWER
   ↓
Feel something about it?    ✗ (no moment exists today)
   ↓
Act again / tell others?    ✗ (no loop exists)
```
**Intervention points (map to the top-3 ideas):**
- Create the *felt moment* at point of sale → **Green Receipt**
- Create the *tell-others* loop → **Seller Badge** (multiplier)
- Create *belonging/pride* → **Impact Counter** (collective)

---

## 6. KEY TENSIONS THE TEAM MUST RESOLVE (great discussion fuel)
1. **Speed vs. message:** the counter moment is fast; the message must not slow shipping. → tiny, passive touchpoints (receipt, screen) beat anything that asks for the customer's time.
2. **Awareness vs. behavior:** is the goal just *knowing*, or *doing more* (GoGreen Plus uptake)? Decide what success means → it sets the KPIs.
3. **Specific vs. emotional:** Green Claims demands specificity; emotion drives memory. → pair a hard number with a human framing ("you cut X kg — thank you").
4. **One persona vs. all four:** focusing on SMEs gives the multiplier; broad gives reach. → recommend SME primary, students secondary.
5. **Compliance vs. boldness:** play it safe and be boring, or be bold and risk a claim misstep. → bold *format*, conservative *claims* (the winning combination).

## 7. "SO WHAT" — WHAT THE SITUATION DEMANDS OF THE CAMPAIGN
A winning idea, given this situation, will:
1. Live at the **point of sale** (that's where the gap is) ✓
2. Make the customer feel like a **participant**, not be lectured ✓
3. Use a **specific, provable number** wrapped in a **human moment** ✓
4. Exploit the **SME multiplier** for free reach ✓
5. Be buildable in **฿5M / 36 branches / 3 months with no celebrities** ✓
6. Visibly **respect Green Claims** (the winner-separator) ✓

→ This is exactly why the **Green Receipt + Seller Badge + Impact Counter** spine fits: it's the
situation's logical conclusion, not a random creative leap.
