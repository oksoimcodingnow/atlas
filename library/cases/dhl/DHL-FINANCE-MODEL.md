## 1. THE FUNNEL (how ฿5M becomes opt-ins → margin)

```
฿5M awareness  →  reach (36 branches × 3 mo)  →  awareness lift  →  GoGreen Plus opt-in  →  DHL margin
                        (survey-measured)         (pre/post)          (~10% conservative)     (high-margin line)
```

**Worked example (conservative, illustrative — label as planning model):**
| Step | Assumption | Source / basis |
|---|---|---|
| Customers reached over 3 mo | ~150,000 walk-in/ServicePoint interactions* | proxy from DHL TH 36-branch footprint |
| Awareness lift (pre→post) | +20 pts (booklet's own method) | brand-lift studies move 2× norm (Lavazza) |
| Opt-in conversion of the aware | ~10% | proven eco-checkout 10–30%, we use the floor |
| → Incremental GoGreen Plus opt-ins | **~3,000 shipments** | 150k × ~20% newly-aware-acting × 10% |

*the exact volume is DHL-internal/not public — we use a transparent proxy and say so.

➡️ **Cost per aware customer** = ฿5,000,000 ÷ (reached × lift%) → the headline efficiency metric, tracked
directly by the pre/post survey. Stating it as a *formula DHL will measure* beats inventing a fake total.

## 2. UNIT ECONOMICS (the "is it worth it?" math)

| Metric | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign cost | ฿5,000,000 | marketing OpEx |
| Carbon cost to DHL | **฿0** | customers fund it via GoGreen Plus (€325/tonne) |
| GoGreen Plus margin | high (premium line) | DHL already built the capability (€7B group CapEx) |
| Cost per incremental opt-in | ฿5M ÷ ~3,000 ≈ **฿1,667** | falls fast as opt-ins scale beyond the pilot |
| Cost per aware customer | ฿5M ÷ (150k × 20%) ≈ **฿167** | the awareness-efficiency number |

**The key financial insight (say this to a bank judge):**
> "We don't model carbon cost — customers fund it. So the ฿5M is pure **customer-acquisition for a
> high-margin product DHL already paid €7B to enable.** Every incremental opt-in is near-pure margin, and
> the pilot's cost-per-opt-in **drops as it scales to all 71 branches.**"

---

## 3. BREAK-EVEN & SCALE LOGIC
- The ฿5M is a **fixed pilot cost** at 36 branches. Awareness + the loyalty loop make opt-in **compounding**,
  not linear (the flywheel + tier repeat-engine).
- **Scale economics:** the build cost (digital, ฿1M) is one-time; rolling to 71 branches roughly doubles
  reach without doubling spend → **cost-per-opt-in falls** at scale. That's the investment case for DHL to
  continue past the pilot.

## 4. SENSITIVITY (defends the "what if your assumption is wrong?" attack)
| Opt-in rate | Incremental opt-ins | Cost / opt-in | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% (pessimistic) | ~600 | ~฿8,300 | still justified — job is **awareness**, opt-in is upside |
| 10% (base) | ~3,000 | ~฿1,667 | the planning case |
| 20% (optimistic) | ~6,000 | ~฿833 | strong; within proven 10–30% range |

➡️ **The ฿5M is justified even at 2%** because its measured deliverable is awareness lift (survey), not
opt-in revenue. Opt-in is the upside, not the break-even. This is the answer to Stress-Test Q1.

## 5. THE MARKET-GAP NUMBER (your friend's killer stat, verified)
- Thai SMEs: **3.26M firms = 99.5% of all businesses · 34.8% of GDP · ~70% of jobs · but only 14.3% of
  exports.** [OSMEP 2024-25]
- **The insight:** 99.5% of businesses produce just 14.3% of exports — **the small are structurally squeezed
  out of cross-border trade.** Our campaign (Seller Badge + cross-border proof) attacks exactly that gap.
- Pitch line: *"99.5% of Thai firms, but 14.3% of exports. The little guy is locked out of cross-border.
  Ship Light hands them a credential that travels — closing that gap is the social ROI on top of the financial."*
