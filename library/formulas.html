Formula quick-ref.

The equations you reach for, typeset on one page. Each one says what it measures and what counts as "good."

All annualizations assume daily data and 252 trading days. Returns are arithmetic unless noted.

Returnsgrowth & compounding
Simple (arithmetic) return
rt = Pt − Pt−1Pt−1
The percentage change over one period. Simple returns add across assets (a portfolio's return is the weighted sum), which is why they're used for cross-sectional work.
Log return
rt = ln(PtPt−1) = ln Pt − ln Pt−1
Logs add across time (sum the daily log returns to get the period return) and are roughly symmetric, so they're the default for time-series modelling. For small moves, log ≈ simple.
Annualized return (geometric)
Rann = (1 + Rperiod)N − 1
Compound a per-period return to a year, where N = periods per year (e.g. 252 daily, 12 monthly). This is CAGR when the period return is the geometric mean per period.
Risk & drawdownhow much it can hurt
Volatility (return standard deviation)
σ = Var(r) , σann = σdaily · √252
Spread of returns around their mean. Volatility scales with the square root of time — that's the √252 factor for daily→annual (it assumes returns are roughly independent across days).
Maximum drawdown
MDD = maxt Peak≤t − VtPeak≤t
The largest peak-to-trough drop in the equity curve, as a fraction of the peak. The pain metric — it answers "what's the worst I'd have sat through?" Drawdown duration (time to recover) matters just as much.
Value at Risk (parametric, normal)
VaRα = −(μ + zα σ)
The loss you won't exceed with confidence 1−α. zα is the normal quantile (z.05 = −1.645, z.01 = −2.326). Caveat: assumes normality, so it understates fat tails. Historical VaR just reads the empirical quantile instead.
Conditional VaR (Expected Shortfall)
CVaRα = E[ loss | loss > VaRα ]
The average loss in the worst α of cases — the size of the tail, not just its edge. More informative than VaR and coherent (it rewards diversification); regulators increasingly prefer it.
Risk-adjustedreturn per unit of risk
Sharpe ratio
S = Rp − Rfσp , Sann = √252 · Sdaily
Excess return per unit of total volatility. Rule of thumb: >1 good, >2 excellent, >3 — suspect a leak or overfit before you believe it.
Sortino ratio
Sortino = Rp − Rfσdown
Like Sharpe, but the denominator is downside deviation — only returns below the target (often 0) count. It stops punishing a strategy for upside volatility. Higher than Sharpe when gains are choppier than losses.
Calmar ratio
Calmar = Rann|MDD|
Annual return per unit of worst drawdown. Speaks to anyone who feels drawdown, not variance. >0.5 is decent, >1 is strong over a full cycle.
Information ratio
IR = Rp − RbTE , TE = σ(Rp − Rb)
Active return over a benchmark per unit of tracking error. The Sharpe of your bets against the index — how skillfully, not just how much, you deviate.
Position sizinghow much to bet
Kelly criterion (discrete bet)
f∗ = bp − qb = p − qb
Fraction of bankroll to wager. p = win prob, q = 1−p, b = net odds received. Maximizes long-run log growth. In practice: bet half-Kelly — nearly all the growth, far less of the wild ride.
Kelly (continuous / investing)
f∗ = μ − rσ2
Optimal leverage = excess return over variance. Note it's σ², not σ — doubling volatility quarters your size. Estimation error in μ is brutal here, which is the real argument for fractional Kelly.
Volatility targeting
wt = σtargetσforecast, t
Scale exposure inversely to forecast vol to hold risk roughly constant: lever up when calm, cut when stormy. Use a lagged forecast (e.g. σ from data up to t−1) or you've peeked at the future.
Relationshipshow things move together
Correlation
ρxy = Cov(x, y)σx σy
Covariance scaled to [−1, 1]. The engine of diversification — combining assets with ρ < 1 lowers portfolio variance. Watch out: correlations drift toward 1 exactly when you need them low (crises).
Beta
β = Cov(ri, rm)Var(rm)
Sensitivity of an asset to the market. β = 1 moves with it; β > 1 amplifies; β < 0 hedges. It's the slope of regressing the asset's returns on the market's.
CAPM expected return
E[Ri] = Rf + βi (E[Rm] − Rf)
Required return = risk-free rate + beta × the equity risk premium. Return above this line is alpha. The baseline every active strategy is implicitly claiming to beat.
Z-score (standardize)
z = x − μσ
How many standard deviations a value sits from its mean. The backbone of mean-reversion signals (fade extreme z) and of normalizing features before a model.
Is it real?don't fool yourself
t-stat of a backtest Sharpe
t ≈ Sann · √years
Rough significance of a Sharpe over a backtest of length years. Reality check: a Sharpe of 1 needs ~4 years just to clear t = 2 — and that's before penalizing for how many strategies you tried. Short, pretty backtests prove almost nothing.
The leakage rule (not a formula, a law)
feature(t) may use data up to t only  ·  label is t → t+1
If a feature can "see" the bar it's predicting, your backtest is fiction. Shift labels with .shift(−1), forecasts with .shift(+1), split in time (never shuffle), and fit scalers on train only. This is the whole ballgame.
