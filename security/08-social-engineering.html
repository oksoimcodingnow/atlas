Lesson 8 · Attacks & defenses

Hacking the human.

The strongest password and the best firewall don't matter if someone just asks you nicely for the keys. The most reliable way into any system isn't a clever exploit — it's a convincing message. This is the attack you personally face most.

~8 min · interactive · the one that targets you directly

Every other lesson defends a machine. This one defends you. Attackers learned long ago that it's far easier to trick a person into opening the door than to break the lock. That's social engineering: manipulating people into giving up access, money, or secrets. And you are the target — not a server.

The con artist's playbook — defined

phishing
A fake message (email, text, DM) impersonating someone you trust — your bank, a delivery service, a boss, Steam — to trick you into clicking a malicious link, entering a password on a fake page, or paying.
spear phishing
Phishing aimed at you specifically, using real details (your name, your employer, a recent purchase) to be far more convincing. The mass-email's sniper cousin.
pretexting
Inventing a believable scenario — "I'm from IT, we detected a problem with your account" — to make the request feel legitimate. The story is the weapon.
the psychological levers
Almost every scam pulls one of four: urgency ("act in 24 hours or lose access"), fear ("suspicious login detected!"), authority ("this is your CEO"), or greed/reward ("you won," "free Robux"). When a message rushes or scares you, that's the tell — slow down because it wants you fast.

Spot the phish

Five messages. For each, decide: legit or phishing? Then I'll show you the tells. Train your eye — this is the single most useful security skill for daily life.

Message 1 of 5
score 0/0

When a message makes you feel urgency, fear, or greed, that feeling is the attack. The cure is a pause: real institutions can wait three minutes while you check.

The defender's habits

Slow down on pressure
Urgency is manufactured. No real bank locks you out for thinking for five minutes. The pause defeats most scams by itself.
Check the sender & the link, don't trust the display
The name shown can be faked; hover/long-press the link to see the real destination. steamcommunlty.com is not steamcommunity.com. When in doubt, navigate to the site yourself — never via their link.
Verify through a second channel
"Your boss" emails asking for gift cards? Call or message them directly. The con relies on you not checking.
MFA is your safety net
Even if you're fooled and give a password, multi-factor auth (Lesson 4) often stops the takeover — the attacker still lacks your second factor. Belt and suspenders.

Recap

The easiest hack is asking. Social engineering targets you, not your machine, pulling urgency, fear, authority, or greed. Defend with a pause, by checking the real sender and link, by verifying through another channel, and by keeping MFA on as the net. The feeling of being rushed is the alarm — listen to it.
