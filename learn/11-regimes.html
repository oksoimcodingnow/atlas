Lesson 11 · Nothing lasts

When the rug gets pulled.

A strategy can print money for two years, then quietly stop, not because it was ever wrong, but because the market changed underneath it. Markets switch between regimes, and a rule that is genius in one is a disaster in the next.

~8 min · why every edge decays · with a regime-flip simulator

The market is not one thing

It looks like a single price chart, but it behaves like several different worlds taking turns. A calm uptrend where prices grind steadily higher. A choppy range where they bounce up and down going nowhere. A panic where everything gaps down at once. Each of these is a regime, and the rules that win are different in each.

This is the practical face of nonstationarity from Lesson 8: the process generating the data is not fixed, it changes. So a model that fit the last regime perfectly can be exactly wrong the moment the regime flips.

Your edge did not break. The world it was built for went away.

Same rule, opposite outcomes

Two of the oldest strategies prove the point, and they are mirror images. Trend following buys what is going up and rides it. It is a hero in a steady trend and gets chopped to pieces in a sideways market, buying every top and selling every bottom. Mean reversion does the opposite: it fades moves, betting prices snap back. It thrives in a choppy range and gets flattened in a strong trend, fighting the move the whole way down.

Neither is better. Each is simply matched to a regime. Run only one and you are making a hidden bet that its regime will last.

regime
A stretch of time where the market behaves in one consistent way (trending, ranging, panicking). Different regimes reward different strategies.
nonstationarity
When the data-generating process itself changes over time. The deeper reason edges decay and backtests go stale.
edge decay
The slow death of a working strategy as the regime shifts or others copy it. Every real edge fades; the question is how fast.
trend following
Buy strength, ride momentum. Wins in sustained trends, loses in choppy ranges.
mean reversion
Fade extremes, bet on snap-back. Wins in ranges, loses badly in strong trends.
regime risk
The danger that you are quietly betting on the current regime lasting. The most common reason a live strategy underperforms its backtest.

You cannot see the flip coming

The hard truth is that regimes do not announce themselves. You usually only know one ended after it has cost you. So the defense is not prediction, it is structure: do not depend on a single regime surviving.

How to survive regime change

1. Diversify across regimes (Lesson 10). Run a trend strategy and a mean-reversion one together, so something works in each world.
2. Re-validate continuously with walk-forward (Lesson 3). If the recent edge is gone, believe the data, not the old backtest.
3. Size down when you are out of regime. Cut risk when your strategy's conditions are absent rather than hoping they return.
4. Expect decay. Assume every edge has a shelf life and keep researching the next one.

Your trend strategy crushed it for three years, then lost money for the last six months while the market went sideways. What happened?

Build for many worlds.

The goal is not a strategy that wins every regime, that does not exist. It is a portfolio where something wins in each.
