Lesson 12 · The capstone

Putting it all together.

Every lesson so far was one gate. This is the gauntlet they form. A trading idea has to survive all of them in a row, and almost none do. Watching a beautiful backtest shrink at every gate is not failure, it is the entire job done correctly.

~9 min · the eleven lessons as one pipeline · with an idea-to-live gauntlet

From idea to live money

Here is the whole course as the order you actually do things. Each step is a previous lesson, and each one can kill the idea:

1
Have an edge, not a prediction. Start with a small repeatable advantage with a reason to exist. Lesson 1
2
Build it without leakage, judge it by risk. Clean features, honest labels, and Sharpe, not just return. Lesson 5 · Lesson 2
3
Validate out-of-sample. Walk-forward on data you never touched, and count your tries so you do not fall for a false positive. Lesson 3 · Lesson 7
4
Charge it for trading. Re-test net of spread, commission, and slippage. Gross is a story. Lesson 9
5
Do not over-power it. Match model capacity to the signal, or it memorizes noise. Lesson 8
6
Size the bet. Turn the surviving edge into position size without over-betting. Lesson 4
7
Diversify and watch the regime. Run uncorrelated edges, and expect this one to decay when the world changes. Lesson 10 · Lesson 6 · Lesson 11
A good backtest is the start of the work, not the end. Everything after it is subtraction.

Every stage takes a cut

This is the part beginners never see. An idea with a gorgeous in-sample Sharpe walks into the pipeline, and each gate shaves it. Overfitting halves it. Searching many variants inflates the winner, so honesty cuts more. Costs subtract a chunk. Trading at real size slips a little more. What limps out the far end is the only number that was ever real.

These are illustrative, typical haircuts, not exact laws. The point is the shape: the bar shrinks at every gate. Push variants up and watch honesty punish the over-searched idea harder.
in-sample vs out-of-sample
In-sample is the data you fit on (flattering). Out-of-sample is fresh data (honest). The gap between them is mostly overfitting.
Sharpe haircut
The rule of thumb that a strategy's live Sharpe is roughly half its in-sample one, before costs even enter. Sometimes worse.
selection bias
The more ideas you try, the more the best one is inflated by luck. The cure is to demand a bigger margin the harder you searched.
deployment threshold
The minimum live Sharpe worth trading after all haircuts. Below it, the edge will not survive a bad year of slippage and decay.

Why this is the actual job

Almost every idea dies somewhere in that funnel, and that is correct. The skill this whole course teaches is not generating signals, it is the honesty to run them through the gauntlet and kill the ones that do not survive. The market pays the disciplined, not the clever. Finding nothing and knowing it beats trading a mirage, every single time.

The deploy checklist

Before any real money: edge has a reason · no leakage · survives walk-forward · survives non-overlapping p-value · profitable net of costs · capacity matches signal · sized sanely · diversified · decay plan in place. Miss one and the funnel will find it for you, with real money.
