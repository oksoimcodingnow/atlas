Lesson 4 · Survival

Sizing the bet.

Here's the cruel twist: you can have a genuine edge and still go broke. Not from being wrong — from betting too big. How much you risk per trade matters as much as whether you're right.

~6 min · interactive · the Kelly criterion

Say you've got a real edge — you win 55% of your bets, even money. You should make money forever, right? Only if you size it right. Bet too little and you crawl. Bet too much and — this is the part that breaks people's brains — you lose money despite winning more than half the time.

From MM2: you know the trader who had good instincts but went all-in on one flip, got unlucky once, and was wiped out. Right call, wrong size. The math of how much is a solved problem, and it has a name.

The terms, defined

position sizing
What fraction of your money you put on a single trade. The most underrated decision in trading — bigger than which asset, bigger than entry timing.
bankroll
Your total capital. Every bet is a fraction of it, and the fraction is what you're choosing.
geometric vs arithmetic growth
Arithmetic = the average of your returns. Geometric = what actually compounds (you multiply, not add). A +50% then −50% averages 0% but leaves you at 0.75× — down 25%. Compounding only cares about the geometric path, and big bets wreck it.
volatility drag
Why the above happens: swings hurt compounding. The bigger your bets, the bigger the swings, the more the drag eats your geometric growth even when the average looks fine.
Kelly criterion
The formula for the bet size that maximizes long-run (geometric) growth. For even-money bets: bet a fraction equal to your edge, (2 × win-rate − 1). A 55% edge → bet 10% of bankroll. Bet more and growth falls; bet double and growth hits zero; beyond that you bleed.
fractional Kelly
What pros actually do: bet half-Kelly or less. You give up a little growth for a lot less stomach-churning volatility, and you survive your model being slightly wrong about its edge.
risk of ruin
The probability you lose so much you can't continue. Over-betting drives it toward 100% — a guaranteed wipeout given enough time, edge or no edge.

An edge tells you which way to bet. Sizing decides whether you compound or detonate. Most pros bet half-Kelly and sleep at night.

For your bot

This is what your bet-sizing skill is for. When a strategy survives walk-forward (Lesson 3) and you know its edge and odds, Kelly tells you the growth-optimal size — and then you bet a fraction of that for safety, because your edge estimate is never exact. Over-sizing a good strategy is one of the most common ways quants lose money.

Recap

Being right is half the battle; sizing is the other half. Kelly finds the bet that compounds fastest, over-betting quietly destroys a real edge through volatility drag, and fractional Kelly is the humble, survivable choice. You can be the best knife-flipper alive and still go broke betting too big.
