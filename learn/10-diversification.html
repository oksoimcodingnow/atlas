Lesson 10 · The only free lunch

The only free lunch.

There is one thing in markets that genuinely gives you something for nothing: combining bets that do not move together. Done right it cuts your risk without cutting your return. It is the closest thing to magic in all of finance, and it has exactly one enemy.

~8 min · why correlation is everything · with a portfolio combiner

One bet is a gamble

You already lived this in MM2. If your entire net worth is one godly knife and that knife crashes, you are wiped out. Spread the same wealth across ten different items and one bad night barely dents you. Same expected gain, far less risk. That instinct is the single most important idea in portfolio building.

In quant terms: one strategy, however good, has a bumpy ride. It has good months and ugly drawdowns. But if you run several strategies whose ups and downs do not line up, their bumps partly cancel. The combined ride is smoother, and a smoother ride at the same return is a higher Sharpe ratio.

Combine bets that move independently and your risk falls while your return stays the same. Nothing else in finance is free.

The magic, in one formula

Take N strategies, each with the same modest Sharpe. If they are perfectly independent, the portfolio's Sharpe is the individual Sharpe times the square root of N. Four independent edges double your Sharpe. Nine of them triple it. You did not improve any single strategy; you just stopped relying on one.

But that only holds while they are independent. The real formula has correlation in it, and correlation is what takes the lunch away.

Combine the edges

Below are N strategies, each the same quality, drawn faint. The bold gold line is the equal-weight portfolio of all of them. correlation
How much two things move together, from -1 (opposite) through 0 (independent) to +1 (identical). The whole game of diversification is finding low-correlation edges.
diversification
Spreading capital across independent bets so no single one can sink you. Reduces risk for the same expected return.
the free lunch
The fact that combining uncorrelated edges raises Sharpe without lowering return. The only genuine free improvement in investing.
concentration risk
The opposite. Too much in one bet, or many bets that are secretly the same bet. Looks diversified, behaves like one position.
sqrt(N) rule
With N independent equal edges, portfolio Sharpe = individual Sharpe times the square root of N. Correlation shrinks the N you effectively have.

Correlation is the enemy

The cruel twist: correlations are not fixed. In calm markets your strategies may look nicely independent. In a crisis, almost everything falls at once. Correlations rush toward 1 exactly when you need diversification most, and the free lunch vanishes on the worst possible day. This is why real risk managers stress-test their portfolios assuming correlations spike, not assuming they stay low.

The rule

Diversification is the only free lunch, but it is paid for in a crisis. Seek edges that are genuinely different in what drives them, not ten versions of the same trade. And never assume the low correlation you measured in calm times will hold when the market panics.

You run ten strategies and your Sharpe is barely higher than running just one. What is the most likely cause?

Don't bet on one knife.

The cheapest way to improve a portfolio is not a better strategy. It is a second strategy that does not move with the first.
