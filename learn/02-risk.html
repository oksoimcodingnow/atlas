Lesson 2 · Foundations

Risk is the real product.

Two strategies can earn the same return. One you'd put your savings in. The other would give you a heart attack. The difference is risk, and there's a single number that captures it.

~6 min · interactive · builds on Lesson 1

In Lesson 1 you learned the goal is a small, real edge. But "I made 40% this year" is a meaningless sentence on its own. 40% how? Smoothly, sleeping well? Or by riding a rollercoaster that was down 60% in the middle and you got lucky it came back?

From MM2: imagine two flippers who both doubled their robux this year. One did it with steady, boring trades. The other YOLO'd everything on one Chroma gamble that happened to pay off. Same return, wildly different skill. If they each did it again, you'd bet on the steady one every time. That instinct is what this lesson makes precise.

The terms, defined

return
How much you made, as a percent. The number everyone brags about, and the least useful on its own.
volatility (σ, "sigma")
How much your returns bounce around — the standard deviation of your daily/periodic returns. High volatility = big swings up and down. It's the textbook measure of risk.
drawdown
How far you've fallen from your peak. If you hit $10k then dropped to $7k, that's a 30% drawdown. Max drawdown is the worst one — the deepest hole you sat in. It's the risk you actually feel.
Sharpe ratio
Return per unit of risk: roughly (your return − the risk-free rate) ÷ volatility. The single most important number in the field. A Sharpe of 1 is good, 2 is excellent, 3+ is suspicious (probably a bug — see Lesson 3). It rewards smooth gains and punishes wild ones.
risk-free rate
What you'd earn doing nothing risky (a government bond, a savings account). Your strategy has to beat this to be worth the stress, so Sharpe subtracts it out.
Sortino ratio
Like Sharpe, but only counts downside volatility. Upside swings don't scare you, so why penalize them? Sortino fixes that.

Same drift, but as volatility rises the Sharpe falls and the drawdown deepens. A strategy returning 40% at a Sharpe of 2 is a different universe from one returning 40% at a Sharpe of 0.4 — the second one will eventually hand you a drawdown that makes you quit at the bottom.

Amateurs chase return. Professionals chase Sharpe — return you can actually survive holding.

Why this matters for your bot

When you compare two alphas, never compare returns. Compare Sharpe. A lower-return, higher-Sharpe strategy is usually the better one: it's steadier, you can lever it up safely, and you won't panic-sell it in a drawdown. Your run_sma.py already prints Sharpe and max drawdown for exactly this reason.

Recap

Return is the headline; risk is the story. Volatility measures the bounce, drawdown measures the pain, and Sharpe ties return and risk into one honest number. From here on, when someone shows you a return, your first question is automatic: "at what Sharpe?"
