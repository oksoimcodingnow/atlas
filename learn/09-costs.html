Lesson 9 · The silent killer

The cost of trading.

Every backtest you have ever seen shows the money you would make if trading were free. It is not. The spread, the commission, and the slippage take a bite out of every single trade, and that bite has quietly killed more strategies than any bad idea ever did.

~8 min · the number that flips winners to losers · with a cost-eats-edge simulator

Gross is not net

You know this one from MM2. The price someone will buy your knife for is lower than the price they will sell it to you for. That gap is the spread, and you pay it on every flip whether you win or lose. Markets are the same, just smaller and faster.

A backtest reports gross returns: the profit if you got every trade at the exact mid price for free. Real life charges you on the way in and the way out. Your true result is net of all that, and net is the only number that ever reaches your wallet.

A strategy is only as good as its edge minus its costs. If the costs are bigger, the edge is a fantasy.

The three bites

Costs come in three flavours, and they all point the same way, against you:

spread
The gap between the best buy and best sell price. You cross it every time you want to trade right now. Half the spread, each side.
commission
The flat fee or percentage your broker or the exchange charges per trade. Small per trade, brutal when you trade a lot.
slippage
The price moves while your order fills, especially if your order is big relative to what is available. You wanted that price; you got a worse one.
turnover
How often you trade. The multiplier on all of the above. Trade twice as often and you pay the costs twice as often.
break-even cost
The cost per trade that exactly cancels your edge per trade. Below it you make money; above it you lose, no matter how clever the signal.

Turnover is the multiplier

Here is the trap that catches fast traders. A signal that earns 6 bps per trade sounds tiny but harmless. Trade it once a week and a 3 bps cost barely matters. Trade it 2,000 times a year and you are paying that cost 2,000 times. The same cost per trade becomes an enormous annual drag. This is why high-frequency strategies live or die on execution, not ideas. Their edge per trade is microscopic, so their costs have to be even smaller.

The rule that saves you

Always backtest net of realistic costs, not gross. Assume the spread, add a commission, and add slippage that grows with your size. If a strategy only works gross, it does not work. The graveyard is full of beautiful gross equity curves that lost money the day they went live.

A quick gut check

net edge per trade = edge - cost
net annual ≈ net edge × trades per year
If net edge per trade is not comfortably positive, no amount of trading volume saves you. Volume just multiplies a negative number.

A strategy backtests at +40% a year gross. After you add realistic costs it is -5%. What is the honest conclusion?

Net, or nothing.

Gross returns are a story. Net returns are the truth. Always ask what a strategy makes after it pays to trade.
