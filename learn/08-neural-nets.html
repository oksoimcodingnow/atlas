Lesson 8 · The capacity trap

Neural nets, and the noise.

You just watched a neural net learn a spiral that no straight line could touch. So why don't quants point one at the market and retire? Because the same power that learns the spiral will happily memorize pure noise, and markets are mostly noise.

~9 min · pairs with the Neural Net Playground · with an overfitting dial you control

The power is real

Open the playground and pick Spiral. A straight line, or even a gentle curve, can never separate two interlocking spirals. The neural net can. Stack enough neurons and a network can bend itself around any shape you show it. Mathematicians proved this: it is called the universal approximation theorem. Given enough capacity, a net can fit essentially any pattern.

That sounds like a cheat code for markets. Feed in prices, let the net find the pattern, get rich. It is the first thing almost everyone tries. It almost never works, and the reason is the most useful idea in this entire course.

A model powerful enough to learn the truth is powerful enough to memorize the noise.

Signal to noise: the whole game

In the playground the dots had a true pattern (the spiral) plus a tiny sprinkle of randomness. The signal was huge and the noise was small, so learning the shape was the easy, correct thing to do.

Markets are the exact opposite. The real, repeatable edge is tiny, and it is buried under an avalanche of randomness, news, panic, and luck. The signal-to-noise ratio is so low that a powerful model, asked to "find the pattern," will mostly find patterns in the noise. It will fit last year's lucky coincidences perfectly and predict nothing about next year.

Turn the power dial

Here is the trap, live. Below are 14 dots: a smooth true trend (the faint line) plus noise. Slide the model power up and watch what happens. Low power underfits and misses the trend. The right power finds it. Crank it up and the curve starts snaking through every single dot: the training error drops toward zero while the honest error, measured on data it never saw, climbs. That gap is overfitting, and it is exactly what a too-big neural net does to a market.

capacity
How flexible a model is, how many shapes it can bend into. More neurons, more layers, higher polynomial degree all mean more capacity.
signal-to-noise ratio
How much real pattern there is versus pure randomness. High in images and language, brutally low in financial prices.
overfitting
When a model memorizes the training data, including its noise, instead of learning the underlying pattern. Train error tiny, real-world error large.
bias-variance tradeoff
Too little capacity = high bias (underfits, misses the trend). Too much = high variance (overfits the noise). The honest error is lowest in between.
nonstationarity
When the pattern itself changes over time. A spiral never moves; a market regime can flip next month, so even a "correct" fit goes stale.

Why markets break neural nets

Three things, all at once, stack against the net:

The three strikes

1. Low signal. There is barely any pattern to find, so capacity gets spent memorizing noise instead.
2. Little data. A net wants millions of clean examples. You have a few thousand noisy, overlapping days (see Lesson 7).
3. It moves. The market is nonstationary. Even a real pattern decays, so a model that fit the past beautifully can be wrong the moment you deploy it.

This is why a simple linear model with a few sensible features often beats a deep neural net on price data. The net's extra power has nothing useful to learn, so it learns the noise. Less power, in a low-signal world, is often more.

When neural nets DO earn their keep

Not never, just not as a magic price oracle. They shine when signal is high and data is huge: reading satellite images of parking lots, parsing earnings-call language, modeling order-book microstructure with millions of events. Used there, as one carefully regularized feature judged by walk-forward (Lesson 3), they help. Pointed raw at a price chart, they overfit.

Your neural net scores 99% accuracy predicting next-day returns on your training data. What does that most likely mean?

Power is not the bottleneck. Signal is.

Go feel it for yourself: train a net on a clean spiral, then remember markets give you neither the clean nor the spiral.
