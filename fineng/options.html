What the price is really saying. An option is the right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) at strike K. Its value splits in two: intrinsic (what you'd get exercising right now) plus time value (the chance it gets better before expiry). More volatility or more time ⇒ more time value, because more can happen. Delta is how much the option price moves per $1 move in the stock — a call's delta runs 0→1, a put's 0→−1.
C = S·N(d₁) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d₂) P = C − S + K·e^(−rT) (put–call parity) d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T] / (σ·√T) d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T
N(·) is the standard-normal CDF — the probability the random walk lands in-the-money. That's the bridge from the Brownian-motion lab to a dollar price.
