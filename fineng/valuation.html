What is it worth?

Two ways to answer. A bond is a fixed stream of cash you discount at the market yield — change the yield and watch price move the opposite way. A stock is an uncertain stream you discount at a higher rate. Same idea, different risk. Play with both.

The see-saw. Coupon is fixed the day the bond is issued. When the market yield rises, your fixed coupons look worse, so the price falls — and vice-versa. That inverse is the whole game. If coupon > yield the bond is above par; if coupon < yield it's below par; equal = exactly par.
P = Σ C/(1+y)ᵗ + F/(1+y)ⁿ (per period: y/m, n = years×m)
Modified duration ≈ % price drop per +1% yield. Longer maturity + lower coupon ⇒ more rate-sensitive.

A stock is a bond with no maturity and uncertain coupons. The simplest fair value is the Gordon growth model: value = next dividend ÷ (discount rate − growth). It only works when r > g — if growth ≥ your discount rate the formula explodes (and so does reality's patience).
Gordon: V = D₁ / (r − g) DCF: V = Σ Dₜ/(1+r)ᵗ + TV/(1+r)ⁿ
Watch the bars: early dividends are worth almost their face; far-future ones shrink hard once discounted. That shrinking is why a high discount rate crushes value.
