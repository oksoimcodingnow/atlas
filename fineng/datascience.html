What returns really look like.

Models love to assume returns are a tidy bell curve. Real markets aren't so polite — they have fat tails: crashes and melt-ups happen far more often than a normal distribution predicts. Generate a return series, watch its histogram, and drag the "tail fatness" to see why "it was a 6-sigma event" is usually a lie about the model, not the market.

Read the numbers like an analyst. Excess kurtosis measures fat tails — it's 0 for a true bell curve; real stock returns run 3–10+. Crank the tail slider and watch it climb while the histogram grows tall spikes near zero and long thin tails. Skewness below zero means the big moves lean to the downside (the equity-market norm). Volatility is annualized by σdaily·√252 — the same √time rule from the Stochastic page. The Sharpe ratio (mean ÷ vol, annualized) is return per unit of risk: the single most-quoted quality number in the field.
