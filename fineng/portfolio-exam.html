Returns

A return is a percentage gain. The one-period version:

R = ( P₁ − P₀ + D ) ÷ P₀

A portfolio's expected return is just the weighted average of its assets:

E(R_p) = Σ wᵢ · E(Rᵢ)

Trap. To annualise, returns scale by ×12, but risk scales by ×√12. Never the same number.

Risk & variance

Variance measures how far returns swing from their average. Standard deviation (σ) is its square root — the number we call "risk."

σ² = Σ (Rᵢ − R̄)² ÷ N
÷ N = population (what your project uses) · ÷ (N−1) = sample

Know which. Your project uses population σ (Excel STDEVP, ÷N) because the 5-year window is treated as the whole dataset.

Covariance & correlation

Covariance asks: do two assets move together? Correlation (ρ) is the same, rescaled to −1…+1 so it's comparable.

σ_AB = Σ (R_A−R̄_A)(R_B−R̄_B) ÷ N
ρ_AB = σ_AB ÷ (σ_A · σ_B)

The whole game. Low correlation is the fuel of diversification — the lower ρ, the more risk cancels when you combine assets.

Two-asset portfolio

Two assets, weights w and (1−w). The exam loves this one:

E(R_p) = w·E(R_A) + (1−w)·E(R_B)

σ²_p = w²σ²_A + (1−w)²σ²_B + 2·w(1−w)·σ_AB

Trap. Keep the 2· on the covariance term, and square the weights. Covariance can be negative — that lowers the portfolio's risk below either asset alone.

Diversification

For an equally-weighted (naïve) portfolio, total variance splits in two:

σ²_p = (1/n)·σ̄² + ((n−1)/n)·σ̄_ij
avg-variance term (→0) avg-covariance term (→ the floor)

As you add stocks, the first term shrinks to zero — that's the diversifiable risk vanishing. The second rises to the average covariance — the undiversifiable floor.

One line. Portfolio risk falls toward the average covariance — you can diversify away company risk, never market risk.

Key. CAPM pays you only for systematic risk. And 1 − R² = the unsystematic share — algebraically identical to the covariance method.

Beta

Beta is how much a stock moves when the market moves. β=1 tracks it; β>1 amplifies; β<1 dampens.

βᵢ = Cov(Rᵢ, R_M) ÷ Var(R_M) (Excel: SLOPE)
Portfolio β = Σ wᵢ·βᵢ

Trap. Beta averages simply across a portfolio; variance does not (it needs the covariances). Only beta is a clean weighted average.

CAPM & the SML

CAPM gives the return an asset should earn for its systematic risk:

E(Rᵢ) = R_f + βᵢ·( R_M − R_f )
yours: 1.57% + β · 7%

Plot that against beta and you get the SML — every fairly-priced asset sits on it. Beating it is alpha (skill or luck).

Don't confuse. SML's x-axis is beta (any asset). CML's x-axis is total σ (efficient portfolios only).

Frontier · MVP · Tangency

Plot every portfolio (x = risk, y = return). The upper-left edge is the efficient frontier.

  • MVP — the leftmost point, lowest possible risk. Minimises variance.
  • Tangency — where a line from R_f just touches the frontier. Highest Sharpe — the optimal risky portfolio.

Long-only. Your project forbids short-selling (weights ≥ 0). Same objective, one extra constraint — so no negative weights.

CAL / CML & Sharpe

The CAL is the line from the risk-free asset through a risky portfolio. Its slope is the Sharpe ratio:

Sharpe = ( E(Rᵖ) − R_f ) ÷ σᵖ

The CML is the steepest possible CAL — the one through the tangency portfolio. Higher Sharpe = steeper line = a better deal.

Market efficiency

The Efficient Market Hypothesis: prices already reflect the information, so you can't reliably beat the market. Three forms:

FormReflects…So no edge from…
Weakpast priceschart / technical analysis
Semi-strongall public infofundamentals on public data
Strongall info, even privateinsider info too

Anomalies (evidence against it): the P/E effect, value (P/BV), size (small caps), January, momentum.

Choice & risk aversion

  • Risk-averse investors need a premium to take on risk — the standard assumption.
  • Utility: U = E(R) − ½·A·σ². Higher A (more risk-averse) → penalise risk more → hold safer mixes.
  • Indifference curves: equal-utility combos of risk and return. The best portfolio is where the highest one touches the CAL.
optimal cash/risky mix: w* = ( E(R_p) − R_f ) ÷ ( A · σ²_p )

Mock exam

Try each before revealing. Bring a calculator.

Q1 · concept

Define systematic and unsystematic risk, and say which one CAPM rewards.

Systematic = market-wide, undiversifiable (e.g. a rate hike). Unsystematic = company-specific, diversifiable (e.g. a recall). CAPM rewards only systematic risk — you could diversify the other away for free.

Q2 · returns

A stock goes ฿40 → ฿44 and pays a ฿2 dividend. Holding-period return?

R = (44 − 40 + 2) ÷ 40 = 15%

Q3 · two-asset

A: E(R)=10%, σ=20%. B: E(R)=16%, σ=30%. ρ=0.2, weights 50/50. Find return and σ.

E(R) = 0.5·10 + 0.5·16 = 13%

σ_AB = 0.2·0.20·0.30 = 0.012
σ²_p = 0.25·0.04 + 0.25·0.09 + 2·0.25·0.012 = 0.0385
σ_p = √0.0385 = 19.6%

Below the 25% simple average — that's the diversification benefit.

Q4 · CAPM

R_f=1.57%, premium=7%, β=1.3. CAPM required return? If it earned 12%, the alpha?

CAPM = 1.57 + 1.3·7 = 10.67%
alpha = 12 − 10.67 = +1.33%

Q5 · EMH

Under semi-strong efficiency, can studying published financials beat the market?

No. Semi-strong means prices already reflect all public info, so fundamental analysis on public data gives no edge.

Q6 · Sharpe

Portfolio: 16% return, 17% σ, R_f=1.57%. Sharpe ratio? What is it, geometrically?

Sharpe = (16 − 1.57) ÷ 17 = 0.85

The slope of the CAL — return earned per unit of total risk.

Q7 · risk split

Total variance 0.09, R² = 0.30. Split into systematic and unsystematic variance.

systematic = 0.30 · 0.09 = 0.027
unsystematic = 0.70 · 0.09 = 0.063

30% market-driven, 70% company-specific.

Q8 · frontier

What is the tangency portfolio, and how does it differ from the MVP?

Tangency = highest-Sharpe portfolio (where a line from R_f touches the frontier) — the optimal risky mix. MVP = lowest-risk point, minimises variance regardless of return, usually a lower Sharpe.

Q9 · diversification

Avg variance 0.008, avg covariance 0.002. Naïve 10-stock total variance, and the undiversifiable %?

total = (1/10)·0.008 + (9/10)·0.002 = 0.0026
undiversifiable = 0.0018 ÷ 0.0026 = 69%

Q10 · concept

Why does portfolio beta average simply, but variance doesn't?

Beta is linear co-movement with one factor, so it weights and adds exactly. Variance carries covariances between every pair — the cross terms don't vanish, so you need the full formula.

Q11 · your project

Explain long-only optimisation, and why the unconstrained tangency had a higher Sharpe.

Long-only = weights ≥ 0 (no short-selling). The unconstrained version may go negative (short), giving it more freedom and a higher — but unrealistic and over-fit — Sharpe. Long-only is the honest, defensible choice.

Common traps

  • Returns scale ×12; risk (σ) scales ×√12. Never the same.
  • 2-asset variance: keep the 2·w(1−w)·σ_AB term, and square the weights.
  • Covariance can be negative — that lowers portfolio risk.
  • SML uses beta (any asset); CML uses total σ (efficient portfolios only).
  • Portfolio beta averages simply; portfolio variance does not.
  • Systematic share = R²; unsystematic = 1 − R² (same as the covariance method).
  • Sharpe uses total σ; Treynor uses beta. Don't swap the denominator.
  • Your constants: R_f = 1.57% (ThaiBMA), premium = 7% (Damodaran), population σ, long-only.
