Fund Mechanics · High-Yield Distributions

Is that yield real, or your own money?

A covered-call or high-dividend fund pays you a fat "yield." But a distribution can only come from two places: the fund's real earnings, or its own capital — draining the NAV. Set a payout rate and how much the fund actually earns, then watch the bucket over time. When the gold line sinks, you're being paid with your own money.

What you're looking at. Each year the fund earns something and pays something. The one rule that decides everything:
NAVnext = NAV + (earnings − fees) − distribution
If the distribution is bigger than what the fund earned, the gap is paid out of the NAV itself — that's return of capital, and the gold line sinks. You still get cash, but each share is now worth less, so the fund must pay you even more next time just to keep the same headline yield. The blue line (NAV + all the cash you collected) is the honest scoreboard: it's your total return. A sky-high yield with a flat blue line means you gained nothing — the fund just shuffled your own money back to you and charged a fee to do it. That is exactly the QYLD / YieldMax pattern.
Return of capital % = capital paid out ÷ total cash collected. Above ~50% is a red flag.
