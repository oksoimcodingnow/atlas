How to drop in your frames

Swap the placeholder.

You just watched a placeholder — a canvas drawing 240 procedural frames so you can verify the scroll mechanic works. To swap in your real Veo-generated frames:

  1. Extract your video to 240 sequential JPGs via EZGif (or any frame extractor) at 30 FPS.
  2. Name them frame-001.jpg through frame-240.jpg (zero-padded to 3 digits).
  3. Drop the folder into demos/frames/ in this repo.
  4. Open showcase.html, find the config block at the top of the script, change MODE = 'procedural' to MODE = 'frames'.
  5. Reload. Done.

If your sequence is more or fewer than 240 frames, change TOTAL_FRAMES too. Everything else (preload, draw, progress bar, overlays) adapts.

A few notes on weight.

240 JPGs at ~300 KB each is ~70 MB. That's a long first paint. Two cures: (1) re-encode the frames smaller — most product shots compress to 150–200 KB with no visible loss; (2) progressive preload — already wired in, frames load as needed first, then in the background.

Why not real 3D?

Real-time 3D (Three.js, WebGL) sounds prestigious but for product shots it gives you worse fidelity than pre-rendered for an order of magnitude more code. Apple, Tesla, every premium product page uses pre-rendered frame sequences. Be honest about what wins.

The trick is the scroll. The work is the frames.
