The choke points — where pricing power lives

ASML · EUV monopoly
The only company that makes extreme-ultraviolet lithography machines. No EUV, no leading-edge chips. The single hardest link to replace.
TSMC · leading edge
Makes the vast majority of the world's most advanced chips. Designers (NVIDIA, Apple) depend on it. Scale + know-how rivals can't quickly copy.
Synopsys + Cadence · EDA duopoly
You cannot design a modern chip without their software. Two firms, deeply embedded, enormous switching costs.

How to find "potential" — honestly

1. Start at the choke points. A wide moat = pricing power = durable profits. ASML, TSMC, and the EDA duopoly are near-irreplaceable. That's structural potential, not a guess.

2. Ride the demand wave, but check the price. AI is pulling demand through the whole chain. But a great company at a crazy price is a bad investment — run the DCF + multiples and demand a margin of safety.

3. Know the cycle. Semis are cyclical — booms and brutal busts (memory especially). "Cheap" at the top of a cycle is a trap; the best entries are when it looks ugly.

4. Don't pretend you can predict the next move. Nobody reliably calls short-term stock direction (we tested it — it's a coin flip). What you can do is buy durable businesses below their worth and wait. That's the edge that's actually real.
